I gave ten AI models one dollar each. Not to chat, and not to answer trivia. Their job was to build a working SQL database server, from an empty folder, in Go, with no libraries.
The best model scored 65.0 points out of 76, and spent $0.99 doing it. The runner-up reached 62.8, and the weakest stopped at 37.
Every query goes to the model's server and to SQLite. DeepSeek wrote its server in Go; Claude Haiku and MiMo chose Python. The grader runs SELECT, JOIN and GROUP BY, and compares every row.
So what does one dollar actually buy? Less than you'd hope. And, in one case, far more than anyone expected.
When the answers differ, even by a single row, in a single query, on a single ticket, the ticket fails, the points are lost, and the model has to decide whether to spend what is left of its dollar fixing the bug or moving on.
It passed twenty tickets. Then it broke its own server, and never got it running again.
There are three things to watch: the score, the cost, and the test coverage. The full results are at subdollarbench.org.
