## Operator decisions

- **D1 (a model's row):**
  - **(a) Recommended:** sort a model's careers with `board_order` and
    show the middle one (the median of 3) as the model's row, with
    best and worst next to it and a `×3 careers ▸` disclosure. No new
    ranking formula.
  - (b) The mean of the careers' points and dollars. That is a new
    formula, with no tie-break defined.
- **D2 (the variance check):**
  - **(a) Recommended:** models are ranked by their median career. Two
    adjacent models are a **tie** when their career ranges overlap: the
    worse model's best career is at least as good as the better model's
    worst, by `board_order`. Ties are published as one rank group
    (`2=`), so the check never blocks publishing; it shapes the ranks.
  - (b) A gate: publishing waits until no adjacent pair overlaps, which
    means more careers.
- **D3 (order of work):**
  - **(a) Recommended:** round 44 first. The operator moves round 40
    back to `draft` until after the grid (it doesn't block cells; the
    reviewer checks the clean tree and the bar by hand).
  - (b) Round 40 first, then 44.

### F2. The results page

A second page, `results.html`, linked from the board. Static HTML,
charts as inline SVG generated in Rust, no JavaScript and no new crate.
Every chart has a table with its numbers next to it.

- **Points against dollars:** one dot per career, coloured by model, with
  the Pareto frontier through the non-dominated careers. Axes are career
  `$` (0 to the career cap of 4 × $0.99) and career points (0 to 76).
- **The survival curve:** P(ticket ≥ i) per model, for i = 0…20,
  averaged over its finished careers. It uses each phase cell's
  per-ticket pass and the same rule as `survival()`.
- **`$` per ticket:** career `$` divided by tickets passed, per model:
  median, min and max.
- **The regression-free rate:** regression-free tickets divided by
  tickets passed, per model.
- **What it measured,** at the top: rules version and fingerprint,
  SQLite version, sandbox image, harness and effort, the cap ($0.99 per
  phase, 4 phases), the grid's dates, the number of careers, and a link
  to the methodology (`docs/PROPOSAL-sdb-sql.md` on GitHub).

Acceptance:
- [ ] On F1's fixture, the frontier contains exactly the non-dominated
  careers — test: `r44_f2_pareto_frontier`.
- [ ] Survival, `$` per ticket and the regression-free rate match
  values computed by hand for the fixture — test: `r44_f2_metrics`.
- [ ] The page names the rules version, the fingerprint and the image —
  test: `r44_f2_results_page_names_what_it_measured`.

### F4. Deploy to the VPS: `scripts/publish.sh`

- **Exports** the portal into a new temporary directory, then checks:
  1. every ranked cell is on the current rules and all of them share one
     fingerprint;
  2. the flake record for that graded content says `RESULT: PASS`;
  3. the export passes the secret scan (`secret_patterns`) with zero
     hits, and contains no `runs/` logs or workspaces;
  4. its size is printed.
- **Pushes** with `rsync -a --delete` to `SDB_PUBLISH_TARGET` from
  `.env` (for example `deploy@host:/var/www/subdollarbench/`). It prints
  `SDB_PUBLISH_URL` at the end.
- **A dry run by default** (`rsync -n`, with the file list). `--yes`
  pushes.
- **Never pushes** without the target set. The script doesn't print
  values from `.env` other than the target and the URL.
- **`README.md`, "Publishing":** the two `.env` keys, a minimal nginx
  `server` block for a static directory, TLS with certbot, and the
  command. The VPS setup is the operator's; the repo only documents it.

Acceptance:
- [ ] `scripts/publish.sh --self-test`, with a fixture export and a
  local target directory, covers:
  - a mixed-fingerprint export: refused;
  - a missing bar: refused;
  - a planted `sk-or-…` string: refused;
  - a dry run: lists the files and copies nothing;
  - `--yes`: copies.

  Check: the output, quoted in the notes.
