## Why

Operator, 2026-11-21: "analyze why we are still having these bugs in
real tests, how to avoid them sooner?", then "96".

Nine bugs since round 85 surfaced in paid runs or in a post-hoc audit,
not in the bar or the rehearsal (the reviewer's table in the chat; in
short: the proxy buffering, the bill missing repair rounds, the coverage
first-binary read, the 256-byte torn tail, ticket-2 syntax in late
checks, model prose tripping the billing fault, the peak window not
matching the grid's model names, the checker's race, and the store
poisoned for a whole career). The common cause: every proof and every
rehearsal grades **one perfect reference server**
(`src/verifier/sql/ref_server.rs`; the rehearsal seeds it,
`src/main_campaign.rs:711-716`) behind **a friendly stub**
(`scripts/opencode-stub.py`), and the rehearsal candidate never fails a
ticket, so rollbacks, catch-ups, store snapshots and chaining after a
failure are never exercised before a paid career. Six of the nine would
have been caught by an imperfect candidate run through a whole career;
two by a hostile stub; one by the real entry point with real names.

## Items

### Z1. A candidate zoo in the rehearsal

The `$0` rehearsal (`scripts/grid.sh --rehearsal`) runs, besides the
reference career, at least five **flawed** candidates through whole
4-phase careers, each with expected per-phase scores and outcomes
written down (an `expected.json` per candidate, like round 91's replay
set):

- **lossy-recovery:** the reference with a recovery that drops data
  written before a kill with an open transaction (mimo `.51`'s bug);
- **compact-and-partial:** a compact append-only log (records under 256
  bytes), ticket 2 not implemented, a test suite that misses one
  feature's regression, a restart slower than 10 s but under 20 s;
- **replay:** the real workspaces in round 91's replay set, chained from
  their phase;
- **lossy-flush** (R95-2): the reference's SIGTERM handler drops the
  last committed rows. The snapshot is taken after the sentinel write's
  graceful stop (`src/pipeline.rs:3205`, `src/main_campaign.rs:1272`),
  but `store_verified` is the closing read before it; expected: the
  ticket whose graceful stop damaged the store fails, the snapshot stays
  at the last verified store, and the next ticket passes;
- **sentinel-write-failure** (R95-3): the reference refuses the
  sentinel's grammar. A failed sentinel write is a warning
  (`src/pipeline.rs:3219`); expected: the ticket fails (the sentinel
  write is part of its contract), the snapshot is not taken, and the
  next ticket starts from the last verified store.

The two new fixtures live under `tests/fixtures/zoo/`, not
`campaigns/` (which is graded), so the fixture files themselves do not
move the key; the fixes in `src/pipeline.rs` do, with `.53`.

A flawed candidate must fail exactly the tickets its flaws earn, recover
through rollback and catch-up where the rules allow, and never fail a
ticket its flaws don't touch. The rehearsal record (`write_rehearsal_record`,
`scripts/grid.sh:405`) is PASS only when every candidate's career matches
its expectation; a mismatch names the candidate, phase and ticket.

Acceptance:
- [ ] Each flawed candidate's career matches its `expected.json` —
      check: the rehearsal record in the notes
- [ ] The lossy-recovery candidate is seen failing the expectation on
      the code before round 95 (its later tickets all fail `broke ticket
      4`) — check: a seen-failing record
- [ ] The lossy-flush and sentinel-write-failure careers match their
      `expected.json` (the ticket that saw the loss fails; the next
      ticket passes) — check: the rehearsal record
- [ ] Both are seen failing on `.52` (the lossy-flush career is
      poisoned; the sentinel-write-failure ticket passes with a
      warning) — check: a seen-failing record each
- [x] A mismatch makes the rehearsal record FAIL and names the ticket —
      test: `r96_z1_zoo_mismatch_fails_the_rehearsal` (self-test PASS,
      seen failing at `302ff6e`)

### Z2. A chaos stub

A second stub mode (`scripts/opencode-stub.py --chaos` or a sibling)
used by tests and by one rehearsal career, which on a fixed schedule:
streams one answer slowly for longer than the agent's 5-minute header
wait; returns a 429, an empty answer and a timeout; writes model text
containing "rate limit", "too many requests" and "unauthorized ... auth";
and is still answering when a session ends (READY, a turn cap).

Acceptance:
- [ ] Through the real billing proxy, every chaos call is billed once and
      the phase bill equals the stub's recorded charges — test:
      `r96_z2_chaos_bill_equals_charges`
- [ ] None of the chaos text trips a billing fault; the 429 and the
      empty answer count in health as their kind — test:
      `r96_z2_chaos_classified_right`
- [ ] Each seen failing on a reverted fix (round 87's buffering, round
      89's prose check, round 90's in-flight wait — any one is enough per
      test)

### Z4. The free-model canary, as a rule and a script

- `scripts/free-canary.sh` runs one phase of a free model (the free
  track's models and pins) on the current code, as a smoke cell on its
  own worker, and writes `sdb-gates/canary-<full key>.txt` with PASS
  when the phase ends `ok` with points > 0 and no checker red flag.

  canary record is missing (`SDB_GRID_NO_CANARY=1` with a reason in the
  log bypasses it, for the operator only).

  version until a free-model canary phase has passed on it".
